# 02 — Literature synthesis

**Method and limits.** This is a targeted search, not a systematic review. Papers are in two tiers. **Tier A** were fetched and read (at least the abstract page) during this work; I list their arXiv IDs. **Tier B** are canonical works cited from my own knowledge; I did **not** re-fetch them and I give no identifiers, so check them before quoting. I could not read any paywalled text. "To my knowledge" below means "I searched and did not find"; absence in a limited search is not proof.

## 1. Tier A (fetched)

| Work | What I took from it |
|---|---|
| Najemnik & Geisler 2005, *Optimal eye movement strategies in visual search* (Nature) | The ideal searcher: a posterior over target locations, a **measured** visibility map `d′(eccentricity)`, evidence combined across fixations, next fixation chosen to maximise probability correct; humans come close. This is the template for RHAN-IM's state and policy. |
| Akbas & Eckstein 2017, *Object detection through search with a foveated visual system* (arXiv 1408.0814) | Ideal-searcher machinery on top of a deep detector with a foveated, eccentricity-dependent visibility; shows the Bayesian state works with learned features. No stopping guarantee, no robustness analysis. |
| Rashidi et al., NeurIPS 2020, *Optimal visual search based on a model of target detectability in natural images* | `d′` maps measured in natural-image backgrounds rather than assumed Gaussian-noise displays: background clutter changes detectability, so it must be measured per condition. |
| Ahmad & Yu 2013, *Active sensing as Bayes-optimal sequential decision-making* (arXiv 1305.6650) | Greedy information gain versus the Bayes-optimal (dynamic-programming) policy for choosing the next sensing action. Our Koopman gain is a greedy one-step rule; optimality is **not** claimed. |
| Naghshvar & Javidi 2013, *Active sequential hypothesis testing* (arXiv 1203.4626) | Posterior-based policies for choosing experiments and stopping, with asymptotic optimality results (Chernoff lineage). Frames "which look next + when to stop" as one sequential test. |
| Wang et al. 2025, *Emulating Human-like Adaptive Vision for Efficient and Flexible Machine Visual Perception* — AdaptiveNN (arXiv 2509.15333) | A modern learned sequence-of-glimpses system (select regions, fuse looks, decide when to stop), trained end-to-end with self-rewarding RL; reports up to 28× lower inference cost at no accuracy loss over 17 benchmarks. **Policy and stopping are learned; no risk guarantee; no adaptive-attack analysis in the abstract.** The strongest *learned* competitor to RHAN-IM. |
| *Sequential Attention-based Sampling for Histopathological Analysis* — SASHA (arXiv 2507.05077, NeurIPS 2025) | Sequential selection of high-resolution tiles for gigapixel pathology. Relevant to the medical branch of the programme; a learned-policy baseline there. |
| Mukherjee, Ibrayev & Roy 2024, *On Inherent Adversarial Robustness of Active Vision Systems* (arXiv 2404.00185) | Active-vision models (GFNet, FALcon) are about 2–3× more robust than a passive CNN against **black-box** attacks. Empirical; no certificate; not adaptive white-box. Motivates RHAN-IM's claim being tested under *adaptive* attacks and backed by a bounded-influence analysis. |
| Jazbec et al. 2024, *Fast yet Safe: Early-Exiting with Risk Control* (arXiv 2405.20915) | Risk-control (Learn-then-Test style) calibration of early-exit thresholds for vision and language. **Consequence: "risk-controlled stopping" is not novel in itself.** What RHAN-IM adds is the pairing with an exact Bayes state and a selective variant (§4). |
| Angelopoulos et al. 2021, *Learn then Test* (arXiv 2110.01052) | Finite-sample, distribution-free risk control by multiple-hypothesis testing over a threshold grid; fixed-sequence testing. The stopping layer of RHAN-IM is an instance. |
| *Clipped BagNet* (Berkeley, IEEE DLS 2020; PDF fetched) and Xiang et al. 2020, *PatchGuard* (arXiv 2005.10884) | Bounded-influence aggregation: local evidence with small receptive fields, clipped or masked before summation, yields provable patch robustness. Source of RHAN-IM's clip `κ`, unit cap and the **local-evidence requirement**. |
| Hong et al. 2020, *A Panda? No, It's a Sloth: Slowdown Attacks on Adaptive Multi-Exit Neural Network Inference* — DeepSloth (arXiv 2010.02432) | Attacks that inflate the *compute* of adaptive networks. Any adaptive-stopping design must be evaluated for compute-exhaustion attacks, not only for accuracy. |
| Huang et al. 2020, *Neural Networks with Recurrent Generative Feedback* — CNN-F (arXiv 2007.09200) | Recurrent generative feedback improves adversarial robustness: evidence that some recurrence helps; mostly empirical, and not an information-acquiring recurrence. |
| Luo et al. 2015, *Foveation-based Mechanisms Alleviate Adversarial Examples* (arXiv 1511.06292) | Foveation as a defence (scale/translation invariance); early support that look structure matters. |
| Singh, Croce & Hein 2023, *Revisiting Adversarial Training for ImageNet* (arXiv 2303.01870) | Modern adversarial-training recipes for ViTs/ConvNeXts: the baseline-recipe reference for any robustness comparison in this project. |
| Geiping et al. 2025, *Scaling up Test-Time Compute with Latent Reasoning: A Recurrent Depth Approach* (arXiv 2502.05171) | Looped recurrent depth gives test-time compute scaling in language models. In vision with a fixed input this is recomputation (see `RHAN_IM_01_FORENSIC_AUDIT.md` F5). Basis of candidate C7. |
| Sun et al. 2024, *Learning to (Learn at Test Time): RNNs with Expressive Hidden States* (arXiv 2407.04620) | Hidden state as a model updated by a learning rule at test time. Basis of candidate C2. |
| Balestriero & LeCun 2026, *Proving and Improving the Platonic Representation Hypothesis with JEPAs* (arXiv 2610.39302) | JEPAs align representations when the paired inputs are two views of the same scene; view quality matters more than scale. Motivates the optional "glimpse-JEPA" objective: two looks of one scene are paired views. |
| DINOv2 repository README | Apache-2.0 licence for the code and weights of the trunk family; the project's trunk is DINOv2-S-shaped with its own training. |

## 3. Synthesis

1. **The recurrent state of an active-perception system is solved on paper, and the solution is a posterior.** Najemnik–Geisler, Akbas–Eckstein, Rashidi et al. all take the state to be a posterior over target locations plus a visibility map that is *measured*. What the machine-learning side (AdaptiveNN, GFNet, FALcon, RAM) replaced it with is a learned policy and a learned fusion. The trade is between *efficiency of a learned policy* and *the interpretability, calibration and auditability of a computed one*. For a project whose differentiator is measurement discipline, the computed one is the natural bet.
2. **Greedy Bayesian lookahead is enough, to the extent tested.** In simulation the closed-form Koopman gain matched an exact Monte-Carlo one-step lookahead (EVOI) within sampling noise (`RHAN_IM_RESULTS.md` A2, B). That is a property of the simulated worlds; optimality over multi-step plans is not claimed (Ahmad & Yu; Naghshvar & Javidi).
3. **Risk control for stopping exists (Jazbec et al.) and works as expected.** The new finding in this project's simulations is not that LTT is valid — that is a theorem — but how badly plug-in thresholds behave when calibration sets are small (27.6 % violation of a 10 % target at n = 300 in the well-specified toy world) and that a *selective* variant is needed when a forced-answer guarantee is unattainable (hard profiles: 100 % abstention).
4. **Bounded influence needs local evidence.** Clipped BagNet and PatchGuard obtain provable patch robustness because every unit of evidence has a small receptive field. A ViT with global attention inside a window mixes all of its tokens, so the *unit of influence is the whole window look*, not the token. This is a design constraint, not a footnote (`RHAN_IM_04_ARCHITECTURE_SPEC.md` §7).
5. **Active vision's empirical robustness (Mukherjee et al.) is under-tested.** Black-box only. The claim "looking strategically is inherently more robust" needs white-box adaptive attacks against the *whole* pipeline (including the policy and the stopping rule) and compute-exhaustion attacks (DeepSloth), in addition to a certificate where one exists.
6. **Recurrence is not one thing.** Feedback that changes the *function* (CNN-F), looped depth (Geiping), and fast-weight state (TTT) all improve what is computed from a fixed input; active perception improves what is *observed*. They answer different questions and need different controls; the Stage-0 NULL belongs to the first family.

## 5. Novelty claim (scoped)

*Not new:* ideal-searcher posterior and visibility map; sequential hypothesis testing; greedy ignorance-reduction policies; Learn-then-Test and its use for early-exit stopping; clipped or masked aggregation for bounded patch influence.

*To my knowledge new (to be re-checked before any publication claim):* using all of them together as the recurrent state, policy, stopping rule and certificate of one vision system whose sensor reliabilities are **measured on the trunk** (including the SDT-adequacy test that decides between "sum" and "best" evidence combination), together with the design constraints that the simulations exposed (local evidence head, `κ` against attainable confidence, unit cap, minimum-looks floor, the vacuity of lossless curtailment at realistic `κ·d`), evaluated under adaptive and compute-exhaustion attacks.

*Not claimed:* that RHAN-IM beats learned glimpse policies (AdaptiveNN) on accuracy per FLOP — that is an experiment (`RHAN_IM_05_EXPERIMENTS.md`, E4); that it is robust — that is E7/E8; anything about natural images beyond the pasted-target stimuli of the gate-1 protocol.
